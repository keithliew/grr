What Is a Guaranteed Rental Return (GRR)?

A plain-English guide for investors who are not lawyers or bankers: what is actually promised, who has to pay it, and what happened to Malaysian owners when the money flowed and when it stopped.

In one sentence: a guaranteed rental return (GRR) is a developer's written promise to pay you a fixed rent on a property you buy, usually a percentage of the price each year, for a set number of years, whether or not the unit earns that much. In Malaysia it is sold mostly with hotel suites, serviced apartments and resort villas. The promise comes from a company, not a bank, and it is only as good as that company's ability to pay.

Amy's Nine Units

In 2000, a woman The Edge called "Amy" and her relatives pooled their savings and bought nine serviced condominium units in Nilai, at RM130,000 each. The developer promised a guaranteed return of 8% to 10% a year for 15 years. The tenants, it said, would be students from the colleges nearby.[9]

Six years later, in May 2006, a letter arrived from the property management company. The guarantee would end in two months. The students, it turned out, had been given hostel places, so the units were hard to let. By 2009 the units had lost almost half their value.[9]

Amy's story is not here to scare you off. GRR can work. It is here because the difference between a good GRR and a bad one is usually hiding in paperwork that most people skim. This guide walks through that paperwork at a speed a busy person can follow.

What Does a Guaranteed Rental Return Mean?

Think of it as renting your unit to the developer. For the first few years the developer is your tenant and pays a fixed rent, guests or no guests. After that the guarantee ends. You get a share of whatever the hotel earns, which can be more or less than the fixed rent, or you take the unit back if your agreement allows it.

1

What you buy

A hotel suite, serviced apartment or resort villa that a hotel operator runs for you.

2

What is promised

A fixed rent, usually a percentage of the price each year, for a set number of years.

3

Who promises

The developer or a company linked to it. No bank stands behind it.

4

What comes after

A share of the hotel's profit, which goes up and down. Or you take the unit back.

Who Pays a Guaranteed Rental Return?

  • A signed agreement. The payer is named in the rental agreement. It may be the developer, a management company or a subsidiary. Check which company signs, then look it up.[1][4]
  • A payer with money. Usually no bank stands behind the promise and no insurer covers it, so ask whether any separate guarantee or insurance exists, and what it covers. "The guarantee is only as good as the financial strength of the company offering it," Paul Khong of Savills Malaysia told The Edge in 2018.[1][11]
  • A hotel that earns. It doesn't change what you are owed, but it decides how easily the payer can keep paying. When guests fill the rooms, the guarantee mostly pays for itself. When they don't, the payer has to dig into its own pocket.

One more thing: the guarantee may already be in the price. A developer can build the cost of the guarantee into the selling price, so part of your "return" may be your own money coming back. Compare the price per square foot with similar finished units nearby.[11][12]

Is GRR Regulated in Malaysia? The Law in Plain Words

What published sources say. Your own agreement, and your lawyer, decide your actual position.

The rental promise is a separate contract from your purchase.

Your sale and purchase agreement (SPA) is covered by Malaysia's housing law, the Housing Development Act, when the project falls under it. The rental agreement that carries the guarantee is a separate contract, and that law does not cover it. As the National House Buyers Association put it, if there is a dispute "it will be brought to the civil court".[2][3][18]

Rent disputes go to the ordinary courts.

The Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims, the low-cost route for housing claims of up to RM50,000, only hears matters under the housing law. Unpaid guaranteed rent is a claim under the rental contract, so owners have taken it to the civil courts instead.[18][19]

The brochure is not the contract.

A "guarantee" in an advert, a brochure or a sales talk does not by itself give you rights you can enforce. Your rights come from the agreement you sign, however glossy the brochure.[1]

The company that pays may not be the developer.

The rental agreement is often signed by a management company or a subsidiary. If that company has little money, winning in court may give you a judgment you cannot collect.[1]

An open question: the Interest Schemes Act 2016.

In 2025 an owners' group, MHO, argued that some guaranteed-return property schemes count as "interest schemes" under this Act, which the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM) oversees. We found no SSM ruling on it either way. Why it matters: if they do count, such schemes would come under SSM's rules. Until then, we know of no regulator that checks a rental promise before it is sold.[6][7]

Went wrong

Nilai serviced condos, Negeri Sembilan

The project: serviced condominiums in Nilai, sold around 2000 at RM130,000 a unit. The GRR: 8% to 10% a year, guaranteed for 15 years. The developer said students from the colleges nearby would rent them. The Edge reported the case in 2009 but did not name the project or the developer.[9]

  1. 2000A family buys 9 units.
  2. May 2006A letter: the guarantee ends in two months. The students had been given hostel places.
  3. 2009The units have lost almost half their value.

Latest: no later report. Because the project was never named, nobody can follow it up, which is a lesson in itself.

So ask who the guests will be, and how much notice the payer must give.

Owners fought back

The Arc @ Cyberjaya, Selangor

The project: about 1,000 serviced apartments in Cyberjaya by Maju Puncakbumi Sdn Bhd, part of Meda Inc. The GRR: promoted from 2011 at 8% a year (gross), paid through the developer's management company, for terms of up to 6 or up to 25 years, as the owner chose.[20][21]

  1. 2014Rent payments begin.
  2. Mid-2016Payments stop for many owners, their lawyer says.
  3. Nov 2017The High Court orders RM3.97 million in unpaid rent, damages and interest for 137 owners.
  4. Jan 2018Another 55 owners sue for RM1.8 million.
  5. May 2018The Court of Appeal dismisses the developer's appeal.

Latest: in October 2018 a new Meda shareholder said he would settle the RM6.79 million owed to owners, and had paid RM771,372.78 so far. We found no later report saying the rest was paid.[5][21]

A signed agreement held up in court, and the owners went together. Winning and being paid are two different days.

Owners fought back

Hatten Square, Melaka

The project: serviced suites and shops on Jalan Merdeka, Melaka, developed by MDSA Development Sdn Bhd. The GRR: owners rented their units to the developer. Suite owners were to be paid every three months for three years from completion (renewable); shop owners for two years.[10]

  1. 2017 to 2021Owners say the contracted rent went unpaid. The developer blamed the Covid lockdowns.
  2. Nov 202278 owners sue.
  3. May 2024The High Court orders about RM2 million in rent, plus RM591,143.90 owners were charged for refurbishments that were never done.

Latest: the May 2024 report does not say whether the developer has paid, and we found no later report.[10]

The developer argued Covid was "force majeure" (events nobody controls). The court found it had no arguable defence. Read what your own agreement says about such events.

Complaint, not a ruling

A KL Sentral project, Kuala Lumpur

The project: units around KL Sentral sold by a local developer that news reports have not named. The GRR: buyers say they were promised 8% to 12% a year. The owners are helped by MHO, a Malaysian community organisation.[6][22]

  1. Aug 2025About 60 investors say they received no returns, cannot use their own units and cannot vote at owners' meetings. MHO calls on the police and the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM) to investigate.
  2. Sep 2025MHO hands SSM a memorandum for 69 owners with purchases of about RM46 million. The owners say the guarantee was never written into their sale agreements, and argue the scheme falls under the Interest Schemes Act 2016, which SSM enforces.
  3. Jun 2026MHO asks the Attorney General's Chambers to direct SSM to finish its investigation.

Latest: no charges, ruling or reply from the developer has been reported. Everything here is the owners' side of the story, and the reports differ slightly on the numbers.[23]

If the guarantee isn't in a document you signed, you may have little to stand on.

Paid as promised, then…

Somerset Puteri Harbour, Johor

The project: serviced residences at Puteri Harbour, Iskandar Puteri, by UMLand, run by the hotel group Ascott. The GRR: 132 of the 204 units were sold with a 10-year leaseback: 5% a year guaranteed for the first two years, then a share of the income.[17]

  1. 2015 to 2017The guaranteed 5% is paid. EdgeProp reported the returns were “promptly paid”.
  2. 2017The guarantee ends. Payments stop for about six months, and owners form a leaseback committee.
  3. 2018One owner works out his return at about 0.5% a year.

Latest: Ascott's 10-year management term was due to end in 2024. We found no report on what owners have received since 2018.[17][24]

The guarantee worked exactly as written. The question that mattered was year three.

If the Rent Stops

What owners in the cases above did, in order. Speak to a lawyer about your own situation.

  1. Read your agreement for who must pay, when, and what happens if they don't.
  2. Ask in writing, and keep copies of everything.
  3. Find the other owners. The Arc and Hatten Square owners went to court as groups of 137 and 78.[5][10]
  4. Send a formal demand through a lawyer, then a civil claim if needed, as The Arc and Hatten Square owners did.[5][10]
  5. Know the limits. A court win is only worth what the paying company can pay.[1]

Questions People Ask

What does GRR stand for?

Guaranteed rental return. The developer, or a company linked to it, promises to pay you a fixed rent for a set number of years after you buy, whatever the property actually earns.

Is a guaranteed rental return really guaranteed?

Only as far as the signed agreement says, and only while the company paying it can pay. It is a company's promise, not insurance and not a bank guarantee.

What happens when the guaranteed years end?

Usually your rent becomes a share of the hotel's profit, which can be higher or lower, or you leave the programme and use, rent out or sell the unit yourself.

How long does a GRR usually last in Malaysia?

It varies. The examples on this page run from two years (Somerset Puteri Harbour) to three years (Royal Lexis and Lexis Hibiscus 2), and one promised 15 years but ended after six. Check the exact start date and end date in your agreement.

Is GRR legal in Malaysia?

Yes. It is an ordinary contract between you and the paying company. The rental agreement is not covered by the Housing Development Act, and whether some schemes count as interest schemes under the Interest Schemes Act 2016 is an open question.

What is the difference between GRR and leaseback?

The words are used loosely. In a leaseback you lease your unit back to the developer or its operator, and the guaranteed rent is often part of that lease. What counts is what your agreement says, whatever it is called.

Has any GRR in Malaysia gone wrong?

Yes. Owners at The Arc @ Cyberjaya and Hatten Square in Melaka went to court over unpaid rent and won judgments, a family in Nilai saw a 15-year guarantee end after six years, and owners at Somerset Puteri Harbour were paid in full for two years and then saw returns fall to about 0.5%. Details and sources are on this page.

Who do I complain to if the rent is not paid?

Start with the company named in your rental agreement, in writing. Owners who were still not paid have gone to the civil courts, often as a group. A lawyer can tell you what your own agreement allows.

No. It is general reference material. Speak to a lawyer or a licensed financial adviser before you make any investment decision.
